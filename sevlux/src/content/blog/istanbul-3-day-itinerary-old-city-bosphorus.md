Three days in Istanbul is enough to experience the city's highlights if you plan wisely. This itinerary balances the must-see historical sites with authentic neighborhood exploration and a taste of the Asian side, ensuring you see Istanbul from multiple perspectives. Day one focuses on the Old City — Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, Topkapi Palace, and the Grand Bazaar. Day two takes you across the Golden Horn to Beyoglu, Galata Tower, Istiklal Street, and a Bosphorus cruise. Day three crosses to the Asian side for Kadikoy's food market, Moda's seaside promenade, and a sunset dinner with Bosphorus views. Each day includes dining recommendations, transport tips, and alternatives for different interests and energy levels.

## Day 1: Old City Wonders (Sultanahmet)

Start your first day early at Hagia Sophia, arriving 15 minutes before opening time (8:45 AM in summer, 8:15 AM in winter). Being among the first visitors gives you 20 minutes of near-solitude in one of the world's greatest interior spaces. Spend about 90 minutes exploring the central dome, the calligraphic medallions, and the upper gallery mosaics. After Hagia Sophia, cross Sultanahmet Square to the Blue Mosque, which opens to visitors after the morning prayer. Spend 30 minutes admiring the Iznik tiles and stained glass windows.

Walk five minutes to Topkapi Palace and arrive around 10:30 AM. Buy the combined ticket that includes Harem access — the Harem is the most beautiful part of the palace and many visitors skip it, so it is less crowded. Spend two hours exploring the courtyards, the Imperial Treasury with the Spoonmaker's Diamond, the Pavilion of the Holy Mantle, and the Harem with its stunning tilework and the Imperial Hall.

For lunch, walk to Sultanahmet's back streets and eat at one of the small lokantas (traditional Turkish restaurants) on Kabasakal Street. Order mercimek corbasi (lentil soup), kofte (meatballs), or a fresh pide (Turkish pizza). Avoid the restaurants directly on the main square, which are overpriced and tourist-oriented. A good lunch here costs 200-300 TL.

In the afternoon, walk 10 minutes to the Basilica Cistern, the 6th-century underground water reservoir with 336 columns and atmospheric lighting. This takes about 45 minutes. Then walk 15 minutes through the historic quarter to the Grand Bazaar, arriving around 2:30 PM. Spend two hours exploring the covered market, bargaining for souvenirs, and soaking in the atmosphere. The bazaar closes at 7:00 PM, but many shops start closing by 6:00 PM.

For dinner, head to Hamdi Restaurant near the Spice Bazaar in Eminonu. This multi-floor restaurant specializes in kebabs and grilled meats, with rooftop seating offering views of the Golden Horn. Try the Adana kebab and the mixed grill platter. Book ahead or arrive early, as it is extremely popular with both locals and tourists.

## Day 2: Beyoglu, Galata, and Bosphorus Cruise

Begin day two by crossing the Golden Horn to the modern side of Istanbul. Take the T1 tram to Eminonu, then walk across the Galata Bridge to Karakoy. The bridge is lined with fishermen and restaurants underneath, and the walk offers great views of the Old City behind you. Stop for a balik ekmek (fish sandwich) from one of the boats docked at Eminonu — a classic Istanbul breakfast-on-the-go.

From Karakoy, walk up to the Galata Tower (or take the historic Tunel funicular for the first stretch). Arrive by 10:00 AM to beat the queues for the observation deck. The 360-degree views from the top are the best in the city. After the tower, explore the Galata neighborhood's cobblestone streets, browsing antique shops and design boutiques. Stop for Turkish coffee at Karabatak, a beloved neighborhood cafe.

Walk down Istiklal Street from the Tunel end toward Taksim. This 1.4-kilometer pedestrian avenue is the heart of modern Istanbul, lined with 19th-century buildings, shops, cafes, churches, and historic passages. Ride the red tram that runs along the center if your legs are tired. Along the way, visit the Flower Passage for a refreshment, and stop at the Mevlevi Lodge Museum to learn about the Whirling Dervishes.

Have lunch at one of the meyhanes (traditional Turkish taverns) in the Asmalimescit area off Istiklal Street. These lively restaurants serve meze (small appetizer plates) with raki, the anise-flavored national drink. Try Sofyalisesi 32 or Asmalimescit 49. Order a selection of hot and cold meze, some grilled fish, and enjoy the boisterous atmosphere. A meyhane lunch with drinks costs about 500-800 TL per person.

In the afternoon, take a Bosphorus cruise. Head back to Eminonu (tram from Karakoy) and board the 2:30 PM public ferry for the long Bosphorus cruise to Anadolu Kavagi. This costs about 150 TL and takes two hours each way, arriving at the fishing village of Anadolu Kavagi near the Black Sea. Have a fish dinner at one of the waterfront restaurants there, then take the return ferry at 6:00 PM, arriving back in Eminonu around 8:00 PM as the city lights come on along the Bosphorus. Alternatively, book a shorter 1.5-hour sightseeing cruise if you prefer to return to the city for dinner.

If you opt for the shorter cruise, end your evening in Beyoglu. The Nevizade Street area and Asmalimescit come alive after dark with outdoor seating, live music, and a festive atmosphere. Have dinner at Zubeyir Ocakbasi, one of the best kebab restaurants in the area, where skewers are grilled over charcoal in an open kitchen.

## Day 3: Asian Side — Kadikoy and Moda

Day three takes you to the Asian side of Istanbul, which many tourists never reach but which offers some of the city's best food, most relaxed atmosphere, and most authentic neighborhoods. Take the T1 tram to Eminonu, then board a ferry to Kadikoy. The 20-minute ferry ride costs about 30 TL with an Istanbulkart and provides stunning views of the Old City, Topkapi Palace, and the Bosphorus. Sit on the right side of the ferry for the best views.

Arrive in Kadikoy and head straight to the Kadikoy Food Market (Carsı Pazar). This is a working market, not a tourist attraction, and it is the best place in Istanbul to see and taste the incredible variety of Turkish food. Stalls overflow with olives, cheeses, honey, dried fruits, nuts, spices, fresh fish, and pickled vegetables. Try sutlu (milky) ice cream from Ali Usta, sample Turkish delight from Kadir Aslan, and buy some cheese and olives from the enthusiastic vendors who offer free samples.

Explore the surrounding streets of Kadikoy, which are filled with vintage shops, record stores, bookshops, and cafes. The neighborhood has a younger, artsier vibe than the European side. Stop for Turkish coffee at Fazil Bey Kahvesi, a legendary coffee shop that has been roasting and grinding beans since 1923. Their Turkish coffee, brewed in copper pots over sand, is considered the best in Istanbul.

Walk 15 minutes from Kadikoy to Moda, a charming seaside neighborhood with tree-lined streets and low-rise apartment buildings. Moda is the most desirable residential area on the Asian side, and its relaxed pace is a welcome contrast to the European side's energy. Walk along the Moda seaside promenade, which curves along the Sea of Marmara with views of the Princes' Islands. Stop at Moda Cay Bahcesi, a tea garden right on the waterfront, for Turkish tea or apple tea with a simit (sesame bread ring).

For lunch, eat at Ciya Sofrasi in Kadikoy, which is widely considered one of the best restaurants in Turkey. Chef Musa Dagdeviren researches and recreates forgotten regional dishes from across Anatolia. The restaurant operates buffet-style — walk along the counter and point to what you want. Try the hummus with pastirma, the various kebabs, the stuffed vegetables, and the dessert selection which includes zerde (saffron rice pudding) and oven-baked pumpkin. A meal here costs 250-400 TL per person.

In the afternoon, take a walk up to the Moda hill or relax in one of the neighborhood's many cafes. If you are feeling energetic, walk to the Kadikoy Bull Statue (the neighborhood's unofficial meeting point) and explore the Bahariye Street tram line, a charming old tram route through a historic commercial district.

For your final dinner in Istanbul, book a table at one of the Bosphorus-side restaurants on the Asian shore. Mikla on the European side gets the fame, but on the Asian side, Develi in Bostanci offers excellent grilled meats and seafood with waterfront views. Alternatively, take the ferry to Bostanci and eat at Kiyi Restaurant, which serves fresh fish with uninterrupted views of the Princes' Islands. End the evening with a ferry ride back to the European side under the stars, with the illuminated city shimmering on both sides of the Bosphorus.

## Where to Eat Each Day

Istanbul's food scene is extraordinary and this itinerary highlights some of the best spots, but here are additional recommendations for each day. On Day 1 in the Old City, do not miss Sultanahmet Koftecisi for kofte and lava beans — a simple, historic restaurant that has been serving since 1920. Also try the Suleymaniye Mosque area, which has several excellent and affordable student-oriented lokantas.

On Day 2 in Beyoglu, start with breakfast at Van Kahvalti Evi on Bogazkesen Street, which serves an authentic Turkish breakfast spread with kaymak (clotted cream) and honey, multiple cheeses, olives, menemen, and unlimited tea. For a sweet treat, have baklava at Karakoy Gulluoglu — the pistachio baklava here is widely considered the best in the world.

On Day 3 on the Asian side, if you cannot get into Ciya Sofrasi, try Baylan, a historic patisserie that has been serving Kadikoy since 1923. Their kup griye (a chocolate and caramel dessert) is legendary. For a casual bite, eat at Halil Lahmacun, which serves the best lahmacun (Turkish-style pizza) on the Asian side.

## Transportation Tips Between Days

The key to efficient Istanbul travel is the Istanbulkart, a rechargeable transit card that works on every form of public transport — trams, metros, buses, ferries, and funiculars. Buy one from any vending machine at a tram or metro station for about 70 TL and load it with at least 200 TL for a three-day visit. Each ride costs 20-30 TL, and transfers between lines within two hours are discounted.

The T1 tram line is your primary transport on Day 1, connecting all the major Old City sites from Eminonu to Sultanahmet and beyond. On Day 2, the tram connects Eminonu to Karakoy, from where you can walk or take the Tunel funicular up to Beyoglu. On Day 3, the tram connects to the Eminonu ferry terminal for the crossing to Kadikoy.

Taxis are an option but use them sparingly. Istanbul traffic can be gridlocked, especially during rush hours (8-10 AM and 5-8 PM), making the tram much faster for Old City areas. If you do take a taxi, insist that the driver uses the meter. The base fare is about 20 TL with a per-kilometer charge. From Sultanahmet to Taksim, expect to pay 80-120 TL depending on traffic. Avoid unlicensed taxis and anyone offering you a "special price" — they will overcharge you.

Walking is an integral part of the Istanbul experience. The Old City sites are all within walking distance of each other, as are the Beyoglu and Galata attractions on Day 2. Wear comfortable shoes — the cobblestone streets and steep hills require sturdy footwear. The total walking distance over three days is approximately 15-20 kilometers, so pace yourself and take breaks at the many cafes and tea gardens along the way.

## Budget Breakdown for 3 Days

A three-day Istanbul trip can be done on a range of budgets. Here is a realistic breakdown for a mid-range traveler. Accommodation in a 3-star hotel in Sultanahmet or Beyoglu costs 1,500-2,500 TL per night, totaling 4,500-7,500 TL for three nights. Food is affordable — budget 400-600 TL per day for three meals, totaling 1,200-1,800 TL.

Attraction costs add up: Hagia Sophia visitor entry 650 TL, Topkapi Palace with Harem 950 TL, Basilica Cistern 300 TL, Galata Tower 650 TL, Bosphorus ferry cruise 150 TL. Total attraction costs: approximately 2,700 TL. Transportation costs about 200 TL for three days with an Istanbulkart. Shopping and souvenirs are variable — budget 500-2,000 TL depending on your appetite for carpets, ceramics, and Turkish delight.

Total estimated cost for three days: 9,100-14,200 TL (roughly 280-440 USD at current exchange rates). Budget travelers can reduce this by staying in hostels (500-800 TL per night), eating at lokantas and street food stalls, and limiting paid attractions. Luxury travelers should multiply the food and accommodation figures by 3-5 times.

## Alternatives for Families with Children

If you are traveling with children, modify the itinerary as follows. On Day 1, shorten the Topkapi Palace visit to one hour (skip the Harem) and spend the extra time at Gülhane Park, which has a large playground and open space for running. The Basilica Cistern is a hit with kids — the underground columns and dramatic lighting feel like an adventure.

On Day 2, skip the Bosphorus long cruise and instead take a shorter 1.5-hour sightseeing cruise, which is enough for children. Visit Miniaturk, a park with miniature models of Turkey's famous landmarks, located near the Golden Horn. Also consider the Istanbul Aquarium in Florya, one of the largest aquariums in Europe.

On Day 3, the Princes' Islands make a great alternative to Kadikoy for families. Take the ferry to Buyukada, rent bicycles, and spend the day car-free. The island has beaches, an ice cream parlor on every corner, and a horse-drawn carriage ride to the hilltop church that children love.

## Packing and Preparation Tips

Pack comfortable walking shoes with good grip — Istanbul's cobblestones and hills demand sturdy footwear. In spring and autumn, bring layers: a light jacket for mornings and evenings, and breathable clothing for midday. In summer, bring sun protection (hat, sunglasses, sunscreen) and a water bottle. In winter, pack a warm coat, scarf, and gloves — the city can be cold and windy, especially near the water.

Women should pack a scarf for mosque visits — one scarf works for all the mosques on this itinerary. Bring a small daypack for carrying water, snacks, and purchases. A portable phone charger is essential, as you will use your phone for navigation, photos, and transit payments throughout the day.

Download offline maps on Google Maps for the Sultanahmet, Beyoglu, and Kadikoy areas before you arrive, so you can navigate without data. Download the Istanbul transit app (Moovit or Citymapper) for real-time transit information. Learn a few basic Turkish phrases — "merhaba" (hello), "tesekkur ederim" (thank you), and "kac para?" (how much?) will take you far.
